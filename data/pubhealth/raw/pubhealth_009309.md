# PubHealth Item 9309

Dataset source id: 9336
Document id: 11379

Fact-check label: true

Evidence and explanation:
The story notes that RU-486 (trade name mifepristone) is being studied as a treatment approach for the prevention of breast cancer in high-risk women, that is, those who carry the BRCA1 gene. The study of this drug is still in an early animal research phase, and due to potential long-term harms of RU-486, this treatment is definitely not for women at this time. The story discusses the study design of RU-486 in a small group of mice. The findings that treated mice (10 mice) did not develop tumors after a year compared to a placebo-treated and control group is interesting and promising, but years of further research are needed before testing could begin in humans. The story cites cancer specialists who emphasize this point. The warning that this treatment is a long way off, if appropriate at all, gives a much needed temperance to the findings of this study and to the overenthusiastic headline of this story. The story does provide a short list of currently available, evidence-based options to lower the risk of breast cancer in women with the BRCA1 gene. This is a very complete story, and it was written in less than 550 words.
