# PubHealth Item 4266

Dataset source id: 4276
Document id: 9366

Fact-check label: false

Evidence and explanation:
Can ketamine help people with depression and other psychiatric diagnoses? Unfortunately, this story doesn’t answer that question, beyond vague claims about one patient’s outcome. It skips any discussion of limitations of the studies or caveats about their conclusions. Importantly, it does not tell readers about the side effects reported in these and other studies. The only two experts quoted are advocates of expanded ketamine use. One of them has applied for a patent on a ketamine delivery device, which is not disclosed. There is little that differentiates this story from others reported by the same journalist on numerous occasions; for example, in early 2012, later in 2012, 2015 and again last year. Following a developing story is a valuable journalistic practice, but there is little in this story that explains why it is news now. The lack of references to side effects or to published studies showing poor results deprive readers of vital background and context. The pain, fear and frustration of psychiatric disorders can be overwhelming, and sufferers and their loved ones may be desperate to find effective help. A story like this one, painting a picture of a miracle cure, preys upon such desperation. Readers deserve a balanced report with appropriate cautions.
