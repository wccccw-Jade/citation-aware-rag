# PubHealth Item 7972

Dataset source id: 7996
Document id: 10364

Fact-check label: false

Evidence and explanation:
This story reports that testing generally healthy people for the presence of calcium deposits on the walls of their coronary arteries may provide clues about who is more likely to die over the following 15 years. That’s an interesting finding that was well worth writing about. However, the story then runs beyond the evidence to feed speculation that coronary artery calcification (CAC) scans could help improve treatments, something this study did not look at. We also have some concerns about the story’s description of the physiological aspects of heart disease. Tests are really only useful when the results make a difference, such as by matching people with appropriate treatments. While this study indicated that, in one group of patients, those with high coronary artery calcification scores were more likely to die over the following 15 years, it did not (and was not capable of) showing that such testing could make any difference in their treatment. News reports should make clear the vital distinction between a test result that can lead to useful action (such as fecal occult blood testing that can help identify treatable colon cancer) and a number like age, that merely predicts the risk of death without offering anything people can do about it.
