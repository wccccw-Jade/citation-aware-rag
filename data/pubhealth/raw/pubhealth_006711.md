# PubHealth Item 6711

Dataset source id: 6735
Document id: 9758

Fact-check label: true

Evidence and explanation:
This story describes a device, ReShape, inserted inside the patient’s stomach that appears to help with weight loss on a short-term temporary basis. Besides the gastric ulcers that struck more than one-third of patients in the study group, we think this device also may also represent a “harm” if it encourages patients down a short-term path rather then helping them make a long-term permanent change in their health. The small study described was financed by the device manufacturer. The story did a good job outlining some of the harms of the device, but the list of potential harms could have been even longer. If someone who is not “medically” obese pays for this device and this procedure and is exposed to risks for only a limited chance at a temporary benefit, is that a harm? This story does not answer that question. Let’s face it, whatever intervention a person undertakes to lose weight, that weight will return when the intervention ends. As a consequence, unless there’s a medical need for rapid weight loss, there’s a limited role for a by-definition temporary intervention, let alone one that confers real risk. So as good a job as the story did in covering the basics of what was behind the FDA’s approval, there could have been – and still could be – an important followup on some of these broader medical/ethical questions.
