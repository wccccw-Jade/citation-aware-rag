# PubHealth Item 2795

Dataset source id: 2805
Document id: 11518

Fact-check label: mixture

Evidence and explanation:
This is a very brief story (156 words), with many of the problems inherent in such briefs. It's about about an over-the-counter product, due out this summer, to help people with weight loss. It is a half strength version of orlistat, a prescription drug, and though the story implies that the weight loss with the over-the-counter version will be about the same as the prescription strength this is not supported by the scientific evidence. And no source of data to support that projection is given. Although the story briefly mentioned clinical trials involving this product and reported on the increased weight loss when using this product in addition to a diet and exercise program, there do not appear to be any studies published in peer reviewed journals. Without that, it is impossible to weigh the strength of the evidence, something the story should have pointed out. In addition, the story did not completely describe the side-effects associated with the use of this product. Overall, no sources were cited. Criticisms of the over-the-counter use of orlistat are easy to find.
