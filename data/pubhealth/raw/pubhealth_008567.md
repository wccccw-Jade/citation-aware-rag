# PubHealth Item 8567

Dataset source id: 8594
Document id: 9529

Fact-check label: true

Evidence and explanation:
This short Time magazine story describes a study that looked at whether the quality of semen could be improved through one of three different exercise approaches, compared to that of men who did not exercise. The story did a good job establishing what made this study unique, and it included a comment from a source independent of the research. But, unfortunately it didn’t delve into enough specifics, such as providing numerical data about the different findings that could help readers better understand the research: What were researchers measuring, and by how much did those measurements change via the intervention? And it left out a very important limitation of the study: It didn’t look at whether these changes had any impact on fertility. The story rightfully points to semen quality as a major factor for couples facing infertility problems–but the study under discussion wasn’t designed to test if exercise has any impact on pregnancy rates. To not mislead readers, any connections drawn between exercise, sperm quality and fertility must be done very cautiously here, and that didn’t happen in this story.
